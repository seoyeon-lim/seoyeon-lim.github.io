Research on housing in macroeconomics draws on two complementary toolkits. Rich household- and loan-level data make careful empirical identification essential. At the same time, studying housing’s macroeconomic implications often requires quantitative models with heterogeneous households, illiquid housing, and mortgage debt, calling for appropriate computational methods. These notes cover empirical identification, models of housing and household finance, and computational methods, with reading notes on research papers connecting these topics.
